## Probate for a Cicero estate

Cicero is a town in Cook County, directly west of Chicago. Cook County estates are filed with the **Probate Division of the Circuit Court of Cook County** at the Richard J. Daley Center, 50 W Washington St, Chicago, IL 60602. The Clerk's probate office is in Room 1202 and the Probate Division is on the 18th floor. Filing is electronic, but the original will is filed in person. There is no separate probate court for Cicero or the western suburbs.

Cook County has the largest probate docket in Illinois, so court dates can take longer to get than in the collar counties.

## Finding an attorney for a Cicero estate

Few law offices in this directory have a Cicero address. That is why this page also shows offices in Berwyn, Oak Park, Forest Park, Riverside, North Riverside, Lyons and Brookfield, in a separate list marked as nearby. The attorney does not have to be in Cicero: what matters is that they handle Cook County probate. The [Chicago page](/city/chicago-probate-attorney) lists more offices a short drive east.

If you need an attorney who speaks a language other than English, ask when you call. Where a listing reports languages, they are shown on the attorney's profile.

## When the estate is mostly the family home

Many Cicero estates come down to one house or two-flat. If it was in the deceased person's name alone, it has to go through probate before it can be sold or transferred, whatever it is worth. Until then the executor is responsible for keeping it insured, secured and maintained. The [guide to selling an inherited house in Illinois](/resources/selling-inherited-property-illinois) covers who can sign and when.

## How long it takes

A straightforward Illinois estate usually takes about 9 to 12 months from opening to closing. It cannot finish much sooner, because creditors get at least six months from the first published notice to file claims. Contested or complex estates can take 12 to 24 months or longer. See the [Illinois probate timeline by county](/resources/illinois-probate-timeline-by-county).

## Is probate needed at all?

Not always. Illinois lets heirs use a small estate affidavit instead of probate when the estate has no real estate and its personal property totals $150,000 or less (for deaths on or after August 15, 2025; $100,000 for earlier deaths). A house in the deceased person's name alone does need probate before it can be sold. [How probate works in Illinois](/resources/how-probate-works-illinois) explains the steps.
