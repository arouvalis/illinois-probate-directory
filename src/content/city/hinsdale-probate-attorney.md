## Which court handles a Hinsdale estate?

Hinsdale sits on the DuPage and Cook county line. An estate is filed in the county where the person lived:

- **DuPage County side** (most of the village): DuPage County Courthouse, 505 N County Farm Rd, Wheaton, IL 60187.
- **Cook County side** (the eastern part): Probate Division, Richard J. Daley Center, 50 W Washington St, Chicago, IL 60602.

If you are not sure, the property tax bill shows the county. An attorney can confirm it quickly. Both courts require electronic filing, and the original will is filed in person.

## Common issues in Hinsdale estates

- **The house.** If it was in the deceased person's name alone, it goes through probate before it can be sold. If it was held in a living trust or in joint tenancy, it passes outside probate.
- **Estate tax.** Illinois taxes estates over $4 million, and the federal estate tax applies to larger estates. Ask the attorney whether a return is needed.
- **Property in more than one state.** A second home elsewhere can mean a second court case in that state.

## How long it takes

A straightforward Illinois estate usually takes about 9 to 12 months from opening to closing. It cannot finish much sooner, because creditors get at least six months from the first published notice to file claims. Contested or complex estates can take 12 to 24 months or longer. See the [Illinois probate timeline by county](/resources/illinois-probate-timeline-by-county).

## Is probate needed at all?

Not always. Illinois lets heirs use a small estate affidavit instead of probate when the estate has no real estate and its personal property totals $150,000 or less (for deaths on or after August 15, 2025; $100,000 for earlier deaths). A house in the deceased person's name alone does need probate before it can be sold. [How probate works in Illinois](/resources/how-probate-works-illinois) explains the steps.
