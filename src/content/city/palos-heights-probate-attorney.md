## Probate for a Palos Heights estate

Palos Heights is in southwest Cook County. Cook County estates are filed with the **Probate Division of the Circuit Court of Cook County** at the Richard J. Daley Center, 50 W Washington St, Chicago, IL 60602. The Clerk's probate office is in Room 1202 and the Probate Division is on the 18th floor. Filing is electronic, but the original will is filed in person.

Cook County has the largest probate docket in Illinois. Many attorneys serving Palos Heights, Palos Hills, Orland Park and Tinley Park file there regularly, even though the courthouse is downtown.

## What the process looks like

1. **Opening the estate.** The executor files a petition. The court admits the will, appoints the executor and issues Letters of Office, the document that gives the executor authority to act for the estate.
2. **Notice to creditors.** A notice is published, and creditors have six months from publication to file claims.
3. **Closing.** After the claims period, the executor pays valid debts, accounts to the heirs and distributes what is left.

## When Palos Heights families usually need an attorney

- **The estate includes a home.** A house in the deceased person's name alone has to go through probate before it can be sold or transferred. This is the most common reason.
- **There is no will,** so the court appoints an administrator and Illinois law decides who inherits.
- **Heirs disagree,** or a creditor files a claim.

Until a house is sold or transferred, the executor is responsible for keeping it insured, secured and maintained. See also [probate attorneys in Orland Park](/city/orland-park-probate-attorney).

## How long it takes

A straightforward Illinois estate usually takes about 9 to 12 months from opening to closing. It cannot finish much sooner, because creditors get at least six months from the first published notice to file claims. Contested or complex estates can take 12 to 24 months or longer. See the [Illinois probate timeline by county](/resources/illinois-probate-timeline-by-county).

## Is probate needed at all?

Not always. Illinois lets heirs use a small estate affidavit instead of probate when the estate has no real estate and its personal property totals $150,000 or less (for deaths on or after August 15, 2025; $100,000 for earlier deaths). A house in the deceased person's name alone does need probate before it can be sold. [How probate works in Illinois](/resources/how-probate-works-illinois) explains the steps.
