## Estate Planning vs. Probate: Understanding the Difference

These two terms are closely related but refer to different things, and it's worth knowing the distinction before you start looking for an attorney.

**Estate planning** happens while you're alive. It involves creating documents — a will, a trust, powers of attorney — that determine what happens to your assets and who makes decisions for you if you become incapacitated. Good estate planning makes things significantly easier for your family after you're gone.

**Probate** is the court process that happens after someone dies. If a person dies with only a will (or no will at all), their estate typically goes through probate — a supervised legal process to settle debts and distribute assets. Probate is public, takes time, and costs money.

The connection: good estate planning can reduce or eliminate the need for probate. A properly funded revocable living trust, for example, allows assets to pass directly to beneficiaries without going through court at all. Many McHenry County families work with an estate planning attorney specifically to avoid putting their heirs through the probate process.

## What Type of Lawyer Do You Need?

After a death, families in McHenry County typically need one of two types of attorneys — and often the same person handles both:

**Probate attorney** — handles the court process of administering a deceased person's estate. This includes filing the petition to open the estate, managing creditor notifications, handling real property transfers, and guiding the executor through to final distribution. If the deceased owned a home or had significant assets, this is who you need.

**Estate planning attorney** — helps living clients create wills, trusts, and powers of attorney. If you're dealing with a death right now, you need a probate attorney. If you're planning ahead for your own family, you need estate planning.

You generally don't need a separate real estate attorney for probate — your probate attorney handles deed transfers and title matters as part of the estate administration.

## What Estate Planning Services Cover

A qualified estate planning attorney in McHenry County can help you with:

**Last will and testament** — designates who receives your assets and, if you have minor children, who becomes their guardian.

**Revocable living trust** — holds your assets during your lifetime and transfers them to beneficiaries after death, bypassing probate entirely. Particularly valuable for families with real estate.

**Power of attorney for finances** — designates someone to manage your financial affairs if you become unable to do so.

**Healthcare power of attorney and living will** — designates someone to make medical decisions on your behalf and documents your wishes about end-of-life care.

**Beneficiary designations review** — retirement accounts and life insurance pass outside of your will, so making sure designations are current and coordinated with your overall plan matters more than most people realize.

## What Makes a Good Probate Attorney in McHenry County

Not all attorneys are equally well-suited for probate work. A few things to look for:

**Illinois licensure and probate focus** — probate law has specific rules, deadlines, and court procedures. An attorney who handles probate regularly will move your case forward more efficiently than a generalist.

**McHenry County Circuit Court familiarity** — probate cases in McHenry County are handled in Woodstock. An attorney who appears there regularly knows the local procedures and requirements.

**Clear communication** — probate takes months, sometimes longer. You want an attorney who explains what's happening at each stage and responds when you have questions. This matters more than most people realize when you're in the middle of a stressful process.

**Transparent fees** — ask about fee structure upfront. In Illinois, probate attorney fees are typically paid from the estate rather than out of pocket, but you should understand what you're agreeing to before signing anything.
