## Probate in Chicago

Every Chicago estate goes through one court. Cook County estates are filed with the **Probate Division of the Circuit Court of Cook County** at the Richard J. Daley Center, 50 W Washington St, Chicago, IL 60602. The Clerk's probate office is in Room 1202 and the Probate Division is on the 18th floor. Filing is electronic, but the original will is filed in person.

Cook County has the largest probate docket in Illinois, so court dates can take longer to get than in the collar counties. See [Illinois probate courts by county](/resources/illinois-probate-courts-by-county) for phone numbers and filing details.

## Where Chicago probate attorneys have their offices

Many of the offices listed here are in the Loop, within a few blocks of the Daley Center. Others are in the neighborhoods, including the Southwest Side, Beverly and Morgan Park, and the North Side. The office location matters less than it seems: most filings are electronic, and a neighborhood office files in the same court as a downtown firm. Each profile shows the address, phone number, hours, Google rating and practice areas so you can check before you call.

This directory lists offices alphabetically. It does not rank attorneys.

## Does the attorney need to be in Chicago?

No. Any Illinois attorney can file in Cook County. If the person who died lived in a suburb, the estate is still filed at the Daley Center, and the [Cook County page](/county/cook) lists suburban offices by town.

## How long it takes

A straightforward Illinois estate usually takes about 9 to 12 months from opening to closing. It cannot finish much sooner, because creditors get at least six months from the first published notice to file claims. Contested or complex estates can take 12 to 24 months or longer. See the [Illinois probate timeline by county](/resources/illinois-probate-timeline-by-county).

## Is probate needed at all?

Not always. Illinois lets heirs use a small estate affidavit instead of probate when the estate has no real estate and its personal property totals $150,000 or less (for deaths on or after August 15, 2025; $100,000 for earlier deaths). A house in the deceased person's name alone does need probate before it can be sold. [How probate works in Illinois](/resources/how-probate-works-illinois) explains the steps.
