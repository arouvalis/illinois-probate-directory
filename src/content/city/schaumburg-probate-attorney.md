## Probate for a Schaumburg estate

Schaumburg is almost entirely in Cook County. Cook County estates are filed with the **Probate Division of the Circuit Court of Cook County** at the Richard J. Daley Center, 50 W Washington St, Chicago, IL 60602. The Clerk's probate office is in Room 1202 and the Probate Division is on the 18th floor. Filing is electronic, but the original will is filed in person.

A small part of the village is in DuPage County. If the person lived on that side, the estate is filed at the DuPage County Courthouse in Wheaton instead. The property tax bill shows the county.

## Finding an attorney for a Schaumburg estate

This page lists offices with a Schaumburg address first, then offices in Hoffman Estates, Rolling Meadows, Roselle, Palatine and Arlington Heights in a separate list marked as nearby. An attorney does not have to be in Schaumburg to file a Schaumburg estate; any Illinois attorney can file in Cook County, and most filings are electronic. A nearby office is mainly a convenience for meetings and for signing documents.

## How long it takes

A straightforward Illinois estate usually takes about 9 to 12 months from opening to closing. It cannot finish much sooner, because creditors get at least six months from the first published notice to file claims. Contested or complex estates can take 12 to 24 months or longer. See the [Illinois probate timeline by county](/resources/illinois-probate-timeline-by-county).

## Is probate needed at all?

Not always. Illinois lets heirs use a small estate affidavit instead of probate when the estate has no real estate and its personal property totals $150,000 or less (for deaths on or after August 15, 2025; $100,000 for earlier deaths). A house in the deceased person's name alone does need probate before it can be sold. [How probate works in Illinois](/resources/how-probate-works-illinois) explains the steps.
