## Which court handles a Burr Ridge estate?

Burr Ridge straddles two counties, and an estate is filed in the county where the person lived:

- **DuPage County side:** DuPage County Courthouse, 505 N County Farm Rd, Wheaton, IL 60187.
- **Cook County side:** Probate Division, Richard J. Daley Center, 50 W Washington St, Chicago, IL 60602.

If you are not sure which applies, the property tax bill shows the county. An attorney can confirm it quickly. Both courts require electronic filing, and the original will is filed in person.

## Finding an attorney for a Burr Ridge estate

This page lists offices with a Burr Ridge address first, then offices in Hinsdale, Willowbrook, Darien, Willow Springs and Western Springs in a separate list marked as nearby. Because the village is split, ask whether the attorney files regularly in the county that will handle your estate.

## What makes Burr Ridge estates different

- **Estate tax.** Illinois taxes estates over $4 million. An estate that combines a house, retirement accounts and investments can reach that level, so ask early whether an Illinois estate tax return is needed.
- **Business interests.** A business owned by the person who died has to be valued and either transferred or wound up. Ask whether the attorney has handled that before.
- **Independent administration.** This is the default in Illinois. It lets the executor act, including selling real estate, without asking the court to approve each step.

## How long it takes

A straightforward Illinois estate usually takes about 9 to 12 months from opening to closing. It cannot finish much sooner, because creditors get at least six months from the first published notice to file claims. Contested or complex estates can take 12 to 24 months or longer. See the [Illinois probate timeline by county](/resources/illinois-probate-timeline-by-county).

## Is probate needed at all?

Not always. Illinois lets heirs use a small estate affidavit instead of probate when the estate has no real estate and its personal property totals $150,000 or less (for deaths on or after August 15, 2025; $100,000 for earlier deaths). A house in the deceased person's name alone does need probate before it can be sold. [How probate works in Illinois](/resources/how-probate-works-illinois) explains the steps.
